What is Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)?

How modern AI systems look up real documents before answering—so they stay accurate, up-to-date, and verifiable.

Knowledge Source: LLM Wiki (5 Ingested Pages) • Use ← / → arrow keys or buttons below to navigate
Source: wiki/01-why-rag.md

1. Why Do Standalone LLMs Need Help?

Frozen Knowledge

An LLM only knows what existed before its training cutoff date. It cannot see today's news or your private notes.

Hallucinations

When unsure, a standalone LLM may guess fluently—sounding confident even when inventing facts.

The RAG Fix

Give the AI an "open-book exam": retrieve relevant notes first, then write the answer from those notes.

Key Analogy: Standalone LLM = closed-book exam from memory. RAG = open-book exam with a fast index.

Source: wiki/index.md

2. The 4-Step RAG Pipeline

Step 1
Chunk

Split long documents into bite-sized pieces.

Step 2
Embed

Convert text chunks into meaning vectors.

Step 3
Retrieve

Find the Top-K closest chunks to the question.

Step 4
Generate

LLM answers strictly using retrieved context.

Steps 1–2 happen offline when preparing your knowledge base; Steps 3–4 happen live whenever a user asks a question.

Source: wiki/02-chunking-strategy.md

3. Step 1: Document Chunking

Embedding a 50-page PDF as a single vector blurs every topic together. Splitting text into focused chunks keeps each piece searchable.

Overlapping Windows

Splitting every ~500 tokens with a 50-token overlap ensures sentences cut at the edge do not lose their context.

Structure-Aware Splits

Splitting by headings or paragraphs keeps complete ideas together—avoiding both tiny fragments and noisy walls of text.

Sources: wiki/03-embeddings-vectors.md & 04-retrieval-ranking.md

4. Steps 2 & 3: Embeddings & Top-K Search

Search by Meaning, Not Exact Words

An embedding model turns text into a list of numbers (a vector). Phrases like "car repair" and "automobile maintenance" sit close together in vector space even without sharing exact words.

Cosine Similarity & Top-K

Cosine similarity measures how close two meaning vectors point (1.0 = identical direction). The database selects the Top-K (e.g., top 3–5) chunks to pass forward.

Source: wiki/05-grounded-generation-eval.md

5. Step 4: Grounded Generation & Checks

System Rule: "Answer using ONLY the retrieved context below. If the answer is missing, say 'I don't know' instead of guessing."

Check 1: Context Relevance

Did the retriever actually fetch the right chunks for the user's question?

Check 2: Faithfulness

Did the final answer stay 100% loyal to those chunks without adding outside claims?

Wrap-Up • Built from LLM Wiki

Key Takeaways

  • RAG solves static memory limits by pairing an LLM with an external, updatable knowledge base.
  • Chunking + Embeddings allow computers to retrieve passages by meaning rather than exact keywords.
  • Quality requires two checks: retrieving relevant evidence and keeping generation strictly grounded in that evidence.
